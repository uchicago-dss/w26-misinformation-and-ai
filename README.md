# w26-misinformation-and-ai

Social media has become one of the primary ways people consume news, but the rise of AI-generated content has made misinformation harder to detect and faster to spread. 

This project investigates how misinformation forms, spreads, and gains legitimacy on social media, with a particular focus on AI-generated content and its interaction with platform algorithms. Members will compare social media discourse on platforms like TikTok, YouTube, and Reddit with official or mainstream news reporting a case study over time. They will then analyze how false or misleading claims emerge, how they evolve, and which narratives are most amplified by tracking keywords, narratives, sentiment, and engagement metrics, and looking for other indicators of misinformation.

By combining text and sentiment analysis, this project asks whether misinformation is systematically rewarded with higher visibility and virality. The findings aim to clarify why misleading content spreads more effectively than verified reporting, and what this implies for platform governance, content moderation, and future policy responses to AI-driven misinformation.
